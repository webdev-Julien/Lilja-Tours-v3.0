## 2. Outreach Strategy (How to Approach Them)

### Core Principles

- **Lead with value, not SEO.**  
  Focus on story quality and fit with their audience, not “guest post” or “backlink”.

- **Match their existing formats:**  
  - Lonely Planet: “Best” lists, destination guides, practical advice. [web:76]  
  - Travel + Leisure: luxury itineraries, features, personal essays. [web:15][web:87]  
  - NatGeo: deeply reported or narrative stories with science/culture. [web:93][web:82]  
  - Iceland blogs & luxury blogs: top‑10s, how‑tos, trip stories.

- **Keep pitches concise:**  
  - Travel + Leisure explicitly wants pitches under 350 words. [web:15]  
  - NatGeo prefers a couple of paragraphs with clear “why now”. [web:93]

- **Present yourself as a local expert:**  
  “Founder of Lilja Tours, an Iceland‑based operator specializing in luxury private tours and small‑group experiences.”

### Basic Pitch Structure

1. **Subject line = mini headline**  
   - `PITCH: Chasing Silence – A 5‑Day Luxury Road Trip in South Iceland`
2. **Hook (1–2 sentences)**  
   - What is the story and why now?
3. **Summary (4–6 sentences)**  
   - Format: guide, top 10, essay, itinerary.  
   - What the reader learns / experiences.
4. **Authority (1–2 sentences)**  
   - Who you are, why you’re qualified (local operator, real trips).
5. **Soft close**  
   - “Would this be a good fit for your [Iceland / luxury / winter travel] coverage?”

## 3. Repurposing Existing Articles Safely

### What Is Safe & Smart

You **can** reuse:

- Topics you already covered on your blog (e.g., luxury Iceland, winter itineraries).  
- Real itineraries and client stories you’ve already implemented.  
- Research, notes, and internal expertise.

You **must** create:

- A **new angle** for each publication.  
- **New wording** (full rewrite), not just minor edits. Search engines and editors want original text. [web:76][web:15]

### Example Reframes

- Blog: “Top 10 Luxury Hotels in Iceland.”  
  - For T+L: “5 Iceland Hotels Where You Can Watch the Northern Lights from Bed.”  
  - For A Luxury Travel Blog: “A Local’s Favorite Design‑Forward Hotels for a Winter Escape in Iceland.”

- Blog: “Winter Road Trip with a U.S. Family.”  
  - For T+L: Itinerary feature: “Chasing Silence: A 5‑Day Private Winter Road Trip in Iceland.”  
  - For Iceland blog: “What One Family Learned Driving Iceland’s South Coast in January.”

### What to Avoid

- Re‑submitting the same text you already published (duplicate content).  
- Sending editors direct links to your blog and asking them to “republish”.  
- Saying “I want to re‑use my blog post” – instead say:

> “I’ve written extensively about this topic for my own clients, but I’d love to offer a **fresh, exclusive angle** for your readers, focusing on [new angle].”

## 4. How to Ask About Backlinks

### When to Bring It Up

- **Not in the first pitch.**  
- After the idea is accepted or in the editing stage.

### Typical Link Options

- **Tier 1 (Lonely Planet, T+L, NatGeo):**  
  - Usually: 1 link in the **author bio** (your site).  
  - In‑body links only when clearly useful (maps, resources), and they may be no‑follow.

- **Tier 2/3 blogs:**  
  - Often allow 1–2 in‑body contextual links + a bio link, as long as they are relevant and not spammy.

### Safe Phrasing – Bio Link

> “I’ll send over a short bio and headshot. Is it okay to include one link to my company site (Lilja Tours) in the bio so readers can find me if they’d like to book similar trips?”

### Safe Phrasing – In‑Body Contextual Link

> “In the itinerary section I mention our ‘Northern Lights Private Tour’. I linked this phrase to the itinerary page so readers can see the route map—please let me know if that fits your style guide, or if you’d prefer to keep links more general.”

If they remove an in‑body link but keep a bio link, the bio link from a high‑authority site is still extremely valuable.
