## Iceland's Newest Landscape, Seen from Above

The **Reykjanes volcanoes helicopter flight** is the shortest and most affordable of our [helicopter flights from Reykjavík](/partner-experiences-iceland/helicopter-tours/), and it covers ground that did not exist a few years ago. Since 2021 a series of eruptions at Fagradalsfjall and along the Sundhnúkur fissure has laid down square kilometres of new lava, built rows of craters and pushed up against the town of Grindavík and the Blue Lagoon. Much of it is closed or hard to reach on foot. From a helicopter, in 30 to 40 minutes, you see all of it.

This is the shared version of the flight, priced per seat with up to four passengers in the helicopter. Every seat has a window. If you would rather have the aircraft to yourselves, the [private Reykjanes volcanoes flight](/partner-experiences-iceland/helicopter-reykjanes-volcano-private/) follows the same route.

## The Route: Fagradalsfjall to the Sundhnúkur Craters

Ten minutes after take-off from Reykjavík Domestic Airport the lava fields begin. The pilot first tracks **Fagradalsfjall**, where the 2021 eruption drew crowds for six months, and the **Meradalir** valley filled by the 2022 flow. Then the route turns toward the **Sundhnúkur crater rows**, source of the eruptions that began in late 2023 and continued through 2026, and the defensive walls built to steer lava away from Grindavík and the Blue Lagoon. Steam still rises from fissures across the fresh fields, and the colours range from matt black to rust where the rock has oxidised.

Visible or flowing lava can never be promised; eruptions start and stop on their own schedule. Even when the ground is quiet, the scale of these landscapes is extraordinary from the air. For the full story of the eruptions, read our article on the [recent volcanic history of Reykjanes](/blog/reykjanes-volcanic-recent-history/), and for a ground-level alternative see the [Reykjanes Peninsula private tour](/private-day-tours-iceland/reykjanes-peninsula/) or the [Fagradalsfjall volcano hike](/private-day-tours-iceland/reykjanes-fagradalsfjall-hike/).

## Weather, Flexibility and What to Expect

The flight has no landing, so sturdy boots are not needed, but warm layers are sensible in any season. The cabin is heated and large coats can travel in the baggage compartment. Icelandic weather changes quickly: if the planned route is closed, the pilot offers an alternative; if a short delay would help, the flight can be postponed by a few hours or to another day, subject to availability; and a full refund applies if the operator has to cancel. Our overview of [how to see lava in Iceland](/blog/how-to-see-lava-in-iceland/) explains when a helicopter is the right tool and when a hike is enough.
