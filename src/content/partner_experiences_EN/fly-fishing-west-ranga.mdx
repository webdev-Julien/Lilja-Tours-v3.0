## Legendary Brown Trout Fishing on West Rangá

The **West Rangá River** stands among Iceland's most celebrated fly fishing destinations, internationally renowned for exceptionally large brown trout. The upper section offers 30 kilometers of exclusive double-bank access, featuring diverse water types from tranquil flat pools to pocket water and fast-water runs.

What distinguishes the West Rangá is the genuine opportunity to land trophy brown trout exceeding 10 pounds—wild, native fish that have thrived for millennia in Iceland's cold, nutrient-rich volcanic waters, developing the strength that makes them legendary worldwide. The river sits along the [South Coast](/blog/ultimate-south-coast-discovery/), one of Iceland's most rewarding regions to explore.

## Private Full-Day Fly Fishing Adventure

This **[exclusive private tour](/private-day-tours-iceland/)** dedicates 8 hours of fishing time within a 10-hour adventure. Your expert guide possesses intimate river knowledge, understanding precisely where large brown trout hold under varying conditions. This expertise transforms random casting into strategic pursuit.

The **private nature** ensures your guide focuses exclusively on your group, adapting techniques and instruction to match your skill level. Whether accomplished angler or eager novice, the day maximizes both enjoyment and likelihood of success across the 30-kilometer stretch.

## Premium Equipment and Complete Service

All **fly fishing equipment** is included: premium rods, reels, lines, leaders, proven fly selections, and quality chest waders. Round-trip transportation from Reykjavík or Selfoss eliminates logistics, and riverside lunch is provided. Simply arrive ready to fish.

## Atlantic Salmon and Autumn Opportunities

**Autumn fishing** offers additional excitement through Atlantic salmon encounters—powerful sea-run fish testing tackle with explosive runs and aerial displays. The possibility of both species adds compelling dimension.

**Varied water types** ensure productive fishing regardless of conditions: flat pools for technical presentations, pocket water for aggressive strikes, and fast-water runs where the river's largest residents hold.
